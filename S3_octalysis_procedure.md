# S3 — Expert Octalysis Assessment: Panel, Instrument, and Procedure

## Judge panel

Four judges participated: **one developer, two technology managers, and one executive leader — including the first author, the only panel member drawn from the artifact's design team**. All had professional experience in software development or software management and prior familiarity with the Octalysis framework. Each judge's sheet recorded an identification block: role (developer / manager / executive / other), years of software-engineering experience, and prior familiarity with Octalysis (none / some / extensive). Judges are referred to as J1–J4 in this package.

The panel was intentionally selected for familiarity with the organizational context and with Octalysis. This strengthens contextual relevance but may introduce shared assumptions; the expert-derived ordering is therefore treated as informed judgment used for triangulation with the IMI evidence, not as a statistical estimate.

## Rating task and scale

Judges rated the alignment of the **techniques in the Octalysis catalog** (92 techniques listed across the eight core drives) with the study objective — *increasing developer engagement in activities that reduce technical debt and improve code quality*. Ratings used a **five-point scale with the anchors: 1 = no alignment, 2 = limited alignment, 3 = moderate alignment, 4 = strong alignment, 5 = total alignment**. (In the consolidated working spreadsheet, entries were recorded as 0–4 and shifted by +1 to the instrument's 1–5 scale prior to aggregation; all reported means are on the 1–5 scale.) Judges could also record open comments per technique.

## Inter-rater agreement (transparency statement)

**Inter-rater agreement statistics cannot be computed for this assessment and are not reported anywhere in the manuscript.** The judges' exercise was consolidated into a single workbook at the time of the study, and rater-aligned disaggregation of the entries was not preserved (one judge's annotated instrument copy survives, which documents the instrument and rating format but does not enable multi-rater coefficients). This is acknowledged as a methodological limitation of the expert-judgment stage. Accordingly, the expert-derived ordering is treated throughout the manuscript as **informed judgment used for triangulation with the IMI evidence** — not as a statistical estimate — and the design constraints derived from it are supported jointly by the two motivational evidence streams rather than by the panel alone. The per-drive dispersion above is provided so readers can inspect the spread of the recorded ratings.

## IMI ↔ Octalysis mapping (design heuristic)

Each judge additionally mapped the four IMI subscales onto conceptually aligned core drives (binary matrices, consolidated by agreement): Interest/Enjoyment → CD3, CD2; Perceived Competence → CD2, CD3; Perceived Choice → CD4, CD1; Pressure/Tension (inverse) → CD6, CD8. This mapping is a documented design heuristic for translating end-user motivational evidence into the intervention design — not a validated psychometric transformation — and is reported as such in the manuscript.
